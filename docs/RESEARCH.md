## 2. What the religion actually is

Victory is **non-denominational charismatic Christianity in the West African (Nigerian)
Pentecostal stream**. It is not Anglican, not Catholic, not Baptist, and not part of the
Alberta-based "Victory Churches International" movement founded by George and Hazel Hill - the
name is a coincidence. This is an independent, immigrant-founded congregation.

### Doctrinal fingerprints, read from their Statement of Faith

Their ten beliefs place them precisely:

- **Belief 3** - the Holy Spirit's "power and gifts are available to believers today."
  This is the **charismatic** marker. Healing, prophecy, tongues, and spiritual gifts are
  understood as live and present, not sealed off in the first century.
- **Belief 10** - the **"Blessing of Abraham"** brings "abundant life" including salvation,
  healing, and **prosperity**. This is the **Word of Faith / prosperity** emphasis, and it is
  the single strongest tell of Nigerian Pentecostal lineage.
- **Belief 4** - the Bible is "the only inspired, infallible, and authoritative Word of God."
  Evangelical biblicism.
- **Belief 9** - water baptism and communion "demonstrate faith but are not essential for
  salvation." Non-sacramental. Ordinances, not sacraments. This is a hard theological line
  away from the Anglican tradition that built their 1856 building.
- **Beliefs 1, 2, 7** - Trinitarian, orthodox Christology, virgin birth, bodily resurrection,
  personal return of Christ in power and glory, resurrection of both saved and lost.

### The lineage behind it

Pentecostalism did not reach Nigeria only as an American export. It rose there in parallel.

- **c. 33 AD - Pentecost, Jerusalem.** Acts 2. The Spirit falls, people speak in languages they
  never learned. The entire movement is named for this day.
- **1906 - Azusa Street, Los Angeles.** William J. Seymour, a Black preacher and the son of
  formerly enslaved parents, leads the revival that launches modern global Pentecostalism.
- **1910-1925 - The Aladura movement, southwestern Nigeria.** *Aladura* is Yoruba for **"the
  praying people."** Indigenous, African-founded churches: the Eternal Sacred Order of the
  Cherubim and Seraphim (1925), the Church of the Lord (Aladura) (1930). Prayer, healing,
  prophecy, led by Africans, not missionaries.
- **1930 - The Joseph Babalola revival at Ilesa.** Thousands converted.
- **1952 - The Redeemed Christian Church of God** founded by Pa Josiah Akindayomi, a former
  Cherubim and Seraphim member. Today one of the largest churches on earth.
- **1970s-80s - Nigerian university campus revivals.** The neo-Pentecostal megachurch era.
  **1986** - David Oyedepo founds Living Faith Church (Winners Chapel).
- **1980s-2000s - Migration.** Nigerian and Ghanaian professionals arrive in Canada. Churches
  are planted in living rooms, basements, rented school gyms and hotel conference rooms
  across the Greater Toronto Area.
- **2006 - Victory begins.** Five people. A living room in Toronto.

### The one story this website should be built around

In the 1800s, Anglican missionaries carried Christianity from Britain into West Africa. In 1856,
Anglicans in the village of Weston, Ontario built **St. John's Church**.

In **2016**, a congregation founded by a Nigerian immigrant took over that building.

Scholars call this **"reverse mission"** - the idea that African churches now re-evangelize the
secularized West. The term is genuinely debated in the academic literature: critics argue these
churches mainly serve their own migrant communities rather than converting host populations, and
that is a fair caution. But whatever one calls it, the address itself tells the story. The faith
went out one way and came back the other, and it now meets in a room built by the people who
first sent it.

**That is the spine of the site. Not a generic church template.**

should look like it belongs to accomplished people.

### Where they are and what they do

Finch-Weston sits in the **Jane-Finch corridor**, one of the most ethnically diverse and most
economically pressured parts of Toronto.

- **StepUp Community Support** (stepuptoserve.com) - weekly food distribution, household
  supplies, counselling and mentorship across the GTA.
- **Nigeria and Philippines** - partnership with local pastors: orphan and senior care, food,
  education, clothing, vocational and life-skills training for youth.
- **Stated expansion** - Ghana, Sudan, Sierra Leone, Jamaica, Haiti, and **First Nations
  communities in Canada**.
- **Ministries** - Men's, Women's, Youth (Sunday + Wednesday Bible study, summer mission trips,
  service projects, retreats), Children's (ages 3-12).

### Worship style

Contemporary. Choir and worship-team led. Expressive. Roughly two hours. Explicitly
"come as you are," no dress code, and they promise a visitor will not be "singled out."
They welcome people from "every nation."

## 5. Design direction that follows from the research

**Do not build a generic church template.** Everything below is derived from the facts above.

### The loading / intro sequence
A **timeline through the years**, which is exactly the mechanism the story needs:
`33 AD Pentecost -> 1856 Weston -> 1906 Azusa -> 1925 Aladura -> 2006 living room, five people
-> 2016 the Anglican church -> today`.
It resolves on the church's own name. It teaches the visitor the whole story in eight seconds.
Must be skippable, must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and must only ever play once per
visitor (store a flag) so returning members are not made to sit through it.

### The homepage structure, borrowing the CollectUI reference
The reference shot uses a **big centered heading, a segmented tab switcher, and one large
rounded window whose contents swap**. That maps directly onto a church:
one frame, tabs for **Sunday / Ministries / Outreach / Victory Life TV**, content swapping
inside. It answers a newcomer's real question - "what actually happens here" - without a scroll.

### Palette
Warm and dignified, not corporate blue and not neon. Deep aubergine/oxblood or a deep
ecclesiastical navy, warm gold as the accent, bone/ivory paper. Gold carries both the Anglican
stained-glass heritage of the building and the celebratory register of West African worship.

### Typography
A high-contrast serif for headings, to hold the 1856 heritage and the gravity of the subject.
A clean neutral sans for body. Large type. Many congregants are older.

### Non-negotiables
- **No em dashes and no emoji anywhere in site copy.** Short dashes and SVG icons only.
- Mobile first. This congregation is overwhelmingly on phones.
- Fast on slow connections.
- Large tap targets, high contrast, generous line height.
- Real photography of the actual congregation, not stock photos of white people in a coffee shop.
