Our history Two thousand years, and twenty Weston Road, Toronto

The long way home

Every church has a history. Ours has two, and they meet at a street corner in Toronto. One begins in Jerusalem and travels through Los Angeles and southwestern Nigeria. The other begins with bricklayers in a small Ontario village in 1856.

The short version

Anglicans built it. A Nigerian pharmacist's congregation filled it.

British missionaries carried Christianity into West Africa in the nineteenth century. In the same century, Anglicans in the Ontario village of Weston built themselves a small red brick church with a cross on the gable.

A hundred and sixty years later, the descendants of that mission field walked in and took up the pews. Scholars have a name for this. They call it reverse mission, and they argue about whether it is real. Standing on Weston Road, the argument feels a little beside the point.

01 The timeline

How the faith reached this corner of Toronto

Gold marks the milestones that belong to this church. The rest is the wider movement that carried it here.

33 AD
Jerusalem

Pentecost

A room full of frightened followers. Wind, fire, and people speaking languages they had never learned. The whole movement takes its name from that day, and every church in this timeline traces itself back to it.

1856
Weston, Canada West

A red brick church goes up on Weston Road

Anglicans in the village of Weston build St. John's: Gothic Revival, pointed arch windows, a rose window over the door and a cross set on the gable. At the time, Britain is sending missionaries in the other direction, into West Africa.

1906
Azusa Street, Los Angeles

The revival that went global

William J. Seymour, a Black preacher and the son of formerly enslaved parents, leads a revival in a converted stable on Azusa Street. Within a few years its influence reaches every continent. Almost every Pentecostal denomination on earth traces a line back to it.

1910 to 1925
Southwestern Nigeria

The Aladura, the praying people

Something rises in Yorubaland that was not imported. Aladura is Yoruba for the praying people. African founded and African led, these churches make prayer, healing and prophecy the centre of worship. The Eternal Sacred Order of the Cherubim and Seraphim is founded in 1925, the Church of the Lord (Aladura) in 1930.

1930
Ilesa, Nigeria

The Babalola revival

Joseph Babalola leads a revival that converts thousands and sets the pattern for Nigerian Christianity for the rest of the century.

1952
Lagos

The Redeemed Christian Church of God

Pa Josiah Akindayomi, a former member of the Cherubim and Seraphim, founds the RCCG. It grows into one of the largest churches on earth, and one of the great engines of Nigerian Christianity abroad.

1970s and 80s
Nigerian universities

The campus revivals

Revival moves through Nigerian universities and produces a generation of educated, urban, entrepreneurial believers. David Oyedepo founds Living Faith Church in 1986. This is the world Pastor Felix Ayomike grows up in before he emigrates.

2006
Toronto

Five people in a living room

Felix Ayomike, a licensed and ordained preacher who arrived in Canada at twenty one and works as a pharmacist, begins holding services at home. Five people. The fellowship outgrows the room and moves into a conference room at the Comfort Inn on Sunday mornings.

2007
2009 Lawrence Ave West

A first worship centre

The congregation takes its own space on Lawrence Avenue West. It fills with young couples from different ethnic backgrounds, and the ministries that still run today begin here.

2016
2125 Weston Road

The 1856 church becomes theirs

The congregation acquires the building that Anglicans raised a hundred and sixty years earlier. The church leadership describes it simply as divine provision. The faith that Britain sent into West Africa comes back through the door of a Canadian parish church, carried by the people it was sent to.

Today
North York, Toronto

Victory Church International

A non denominational, multicultural congregation with roots in Nigeria, Ghana, the Philippines and the Caribbean. Thirteen ministry leaders who are also nurses, doctors, pharmacists, lawyers and professors. Food distributed every week across the Greater Toronto Area. Partners in Nigeria and the Philippines. Love God. Love People. Pray.

02 A note on the term

What people mean by reverse mission

For two centuries, missionaries travelled from Europe and North America to Africa. Today the largest and fastest growing churches in the world are African, and their congregations are planting churches in London, New York, Ottawa and Toronto. Scholars call this reverse mission.

It is a contested idea, and honestly so. Researchers studying African Pentecostal churches in Canada have pointed out that most of these congregations mostly serve their own migrant communities rather than converting the wider society, and that calling it a re evangelisation of the West overstates the case.

Whatever the right word is, the plain facts stand. A church built by Anglicans in 1856 is now filled on Sunday mornings by a congregation from Lagos, Accra, Manila and Kingston. The building did not move. The faith went around the world and came back.

For this congregation the question is less academic than practical. There is a food bank to run on Weston Road, orphans to school in Nigeria, and a service to lead on Sunday at half past ten.
